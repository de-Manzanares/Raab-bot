# Raab-bot-v2

A complete rewrite of my first and very terrible chess engine to be my second and almost decent chess engine.

None of the theory herein is my own; merely the clumsy and approximate implementations of said theory.
I am grateful to the many thinkers and authors that have contributed to the chess programming corpus.
It has been a lot of fun trying to understand their ideas.

## Engine Features

- [0x88 board](https://www.chessprogramming.org/0x88)
- search
    - [alpha-beta](https://www.chessprogramming.org/Alpha-Beta) with [principal variation search](https://www.chessprogramming.org/Principal_Variation_Search)
    - [futility pruning](https://www.chessprogramming.org/Futility_Pruning)
    - [history heuristic](https://www.chessprogramming.org/History_Heuristic)
    - [iterative deepening](https://www.chessprogramming.org/Iterative_Deepening)
    - [killer move heuristic](https://www.chessprogramming.org/Killer_Heuristic)
    - [null move pruning](https://www.chessprogramming.org/Null_Move_Pruning)
    - [quiescence search](https://www.chessprogramming.org/Quiescence_Search) with [delta pruning](https://www.chessprogramming.org/Delta_Pruning)
    - [razoring](https://www.chessprogramming.org/Razoring)
    - [transposition table](https://www.chessprogramming.org/Transposition_Table)
- evaluation
    - Tomasz Michniewski's [simplified evaluation function](https://www.chessprogramming.org/Simplified_Evaluation_Function)
- protocol
    - [UCI](https://www.chessprogramming.org/UCI)

## Some comments on ratings

In the Elo rating system a rating difference of $400r$ points, where $r\in\mathbb{R}_0^+$, corresponds to an expected score of $10^r:1$.
For example, if two players have a rating difference of $800$ points, we can expect the higher rated opponent to win $100:1$.
Looking at $\text{Eq2}$ we can see the term responsible for this: $10^{-R_{AB}/400}$. Very cool!

The difference in rating between players $A$ and $B$, $R_{AB}=R_A-R_B$, as a function of player $A$'s performance against player $B$, say $E_{AB}$, is

$$
R_{AB}=-400\log_{10}\left(E_{AB}^{-1}-1\right).\tag{Eq1}
$$

For example, if player $A$ is scoring $75\%$ against player $B$, we can estimate their rating to be $- 400 \log_{10}\left(0.75^{-1}-1\right)\approx +191$ points from player $B$'s rating.

Player $A$'s expected performance against player $B$, $E_{AB}$, as a function of $R_{AB}$ is

$$
E_{AB}=\left(1+10^{-R_{AB}/400}\right)^{-1}.\tag{Eq2}
$$

For example, if player $A$'s rating is $191$ points lower than player $B$'s rating, player $A$ can expect to score $\left(1+10^{-(-191)/400}\right)^{-1}\approx25\%$.

The above equations describe the Elo rating system exactly and the Glicko rating system approximately; approximately because the equations ignore Glicko's rating deviation terms.
The proper relation between expected outcome and rating difference within the Glicko system can be found on page 5 of [this paper](https://web.archive.org/web/20221103170241/http://glicko.net/glicko/glicko.pdf).
